# Using the JSK CRM — a guide for the team

This guide is for everyone who uses the CRM: salespeople, managers, the owner and
the administrator. It assumes no technical knowledge.

You can practise everything here in the **training system**, which is filled with
made-up customers. Anything with an orange **DEMO / TRAINING DATA** bar across the
top is practice. If there is no orange bar, you are in the real system and real
customers will see the results.

---

## 1. What this system is for

One thing, above everything else:

> **So that no customer enquiry is ever forgotten.**

A customer walks in, asks about tiles for their new house, and says "I'll think
about it." If nobody calls them back on Thursday, that sale goes to the shop down
the road — not because the price was wrong, but because nobody remembered.

The CRM remembers for you. That is why almost every screen asks you the same
question: **what is the next thing you will do for this customer, and when?**

---

## 2. Signing in

1. Open the CRM address in your phone or computer browser.
2. Enter your email address and password.
3. Tap **Sign in**.

You cannot create your own account. The owner or the administrator creates it for
you. If you forget your password, ask the administrator to reset it.

If you sign in and see almost nothing, that is usually correct — a salesperson
sees their own customers, not everybody's.

---

## 3. Your day: the Today screen

**Today** is the first screen you should open every morning. It is your work list.

It shows four groups:

| Group | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Overdue** | You promised to do something and the day has passed | Do these first |
| **Due today** | You planned to do it today | Do these next |
| **Missing next action** | An open enquiry with no plan at all | Decide the next step and set a date |
| **Upcoming** | Coming up in the next few days | Look ahead, plan your route |

**If Today is empty, you are finished — that is the whole idea.**

An enquiry with no next action is the dangerous one. It is not lost yet, but
nobody has decided anything, and that is how enquiries quietly die.

## 4. The salesperson's workflow

### 4.1 Add a new customer

1. Tap the big **+** button at the bottom of the screen (or **New customer** on a
   computer).
2. Enter the **name** and the **phone number**. Those two are enough to start.
3. Choose the **customer type** — homeowner, contractor, architect, builder.
4. Choose the **city** and, if you know it, the **area**.
5. Tap **Save**.

If someone has already added that phone number, the system warns you before you
create a second copy of the same customer. Check the warning — one customer with a
full history is worth far more than two half-empty records.

### 4.2 Add an enquiry (an opportunity)

A customer is a person. An **enquiry** is a thing they might buy. The same
customer can have several enquiries over the years — that is the point.

1. Open the customer.
2. Tap **New enquiry**.
3. Fill in:
   - **What they are looking at** — tiles, marble, granite, sanitaryware, and so on
   - **Roughly how much it is worth** — an estimate is fine, it sharpens later
   - **Where it came from** — walk-in, phone, a referral from an architect
4. Set the **next action** and its **date**. Do not skip this.
5. Tap **Save**.

### 4.3 Log what happened

Every time you speak to the customer, record it. It takes fifteen seconds and it
is what makes the whole system worth using.

1. Open the enquiry (or the customer).
2. Tap **Log activity**.
3. Choose the **type** — call, WhatsApp, showroom visit, site visit, meeting,
   email, note.
4. Write one line about what happened. Plain language. "Wants a lighter shade,
   will decide after speaking to his wife" is a perfect entry.
5. Choose the **outcome** — positive, neutral, negative, no response.
6. **Set the next action and date before you save.**

You can correct an activity for **24 hours** after writing it. After that it is
part of the history and cannot be changed — if something was wrong, add a new note
explaining it. This is deliberate: the history has to be trustworthy.

### 4.4 Set the next follow-up

This is the single most important habit.

Every time you finish talking to a customer, answer two questions:

- **What is the next thing I will do?** (call, send a quotation, arrange a site
  visit, share samples)
- **When?** (a real date)

Enter both. The enquiry then appears on your Today screen on that date. That is
the promise the system keeps for you.

### 4.5 Move the enquiry along

As the customer gets closer to buying, move the enquiry to the next stage:

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **New** | Just came in, not yet properly discussed |
| **Qualified** | Real requirement, real budget, worth your time |
| **Selection** | Choosing designs and materials |
| **Quoted** | You have given them a written quotation |
| **Negotiation** | Talking about price and terms |
| **Verbal confirmation** | They have said yes, but nothing is on paper |
| **Won** | Order confirmed |
| **Lost** | They bought elsewhere or dropped the plan |
| **Nurture** | Genuine, but not now — come back later |

Some rules the system enforces:

- To mark an enquiry **Quoted**, you must enter the quotation reference, value and
  date. A quotation nobody can find is not a quotation.
- To mark it **Won**, you must enter the final order value.
- To mark it **Lost**, you must choose a reason. This is not blame — it is how the
  owner finds out we are losing on delivery time rather than price.
- **Nurture** always needs a date to come back to it, or it becomes a graveyard.

### 4.6 Add a site photo or a quotation file

1. Open the enquiry.
2. Tap **Attach**.
3. Take the photo, or choose the file.

Photographs of the site, the existing flooring or the sample laid out on the floor
are worth more than any note. Up to 10 MB per file.

### 4.7 WhatsApp a customer

Tap the WhatsApp icon next to their number. It opens WhatsApp with the customer
ready. Send the message yourself — the CRM never sends anything to a customer on
its own, so nothing goes out in your name that you did not write.

## 5. The manager's workflow

You see everything for the showroom or showrooms you manage.

### 5.1 Every morning

Open **Today**, then the **Dashboard**. Look for:

- **Overdue follow-ups** — who is falling behind, and on which customers
- **Missing next action** — enquiries with no plan at all
- **Stalled enquiries** — sitting in the same stage far too long
- **New enquiries not yet touched** — the SLA flag; a new enquiry nobody has
  called is the fastest way to lose one

### 5.2 Every week

- **Team** — each salesperson's open work, what they won, what they lost
- **Reports → Pipeline** — what is in the pipeline and at which stage
- **Reports → Won/Lost** and **Lost reasons** — why we are losing
- **Reports → At risk** — good enquiries going quiet
- **Reports → Targets** — target against actual

### 5.3 Reassigning work

When somebody leaves or goes on long leave, open their work and reassign it. The
history stays with the person who did it — reassignment changes who is
responsible from now on, not who made the calls last month.

## 6. The owner's view

You see everything, across every showroom.

- **Dashboard** — the whole business at a glance
- **Reports → Outlets** — showroom against showroom
- **Reports → Salespeople** — performance across the team
- **Reports → Conversion** — how many quotations turn into orders
- **Reports → Customers** — who buys repeatedly
- **Settings** — the numbers the system runs on

You also receive a daily summary by email, if email is switched on. **The CRM
works perfectly well without email** — the summary is a convenience, and every
number in it is on the dashboard.

### What you can change in Settings

Without anyone touching the software:

- the list of **cities** and **material types**
- **stage probabilities** — how likely each stage is to close
- the **high-value threshold** — what counts as a big enquiry
- **dormancy** and **stalled** thresholds — how long is too long
- the **new-enquiry SLA** — how quickly a new enquiry must be touched
- when the **owner summary** is sent

---

## 7. The administrator's job

The administrator manages **people and settings, not customers**. You deliberately
cannot see customer records — that is a salesperson's and manager's work.

- **Create a user** — name, email, role, and which showroom they work at
- **Deactivate a user** — when someone leaves. Never delete: their history stays
- **Reset a password**
- **Set outlet scope** — which showrooms a manager oversees

> **Every manager must be given at least one showroom.** A manager with no
> showroom sees no data at all, and it looks like a broken login rather than a
> permission setting.

## 8. Importing existing customer data

If you have customers in a spreadsheet:

1. Go to **Import**.
2. Download the **template** for customers or contacts.
3. Copy your data into the template's columns. Do not rename the columns.
4. Upload it.
5. **Review the preview.** The system flags problems before anything is saved:
   - rows with errors that cannot be imported
   - rows that look like customers you already have
6. Fix what you can, decide what to skip, then confirm.

Nothing is written until you confirm. If an import goes wrong, it can be rolled
back.

Only customers and contacts can be imported. Enquiries and projects are entered as
they happen.

## 9. Archive and merge

### Removing a record

Nothing is ever really deleted. **Remove** means **archive**: the record leaves
the everyday lists and stops counting towards the pipeline, but its history is
kept and it can be brought back. Archived records live under **Archive**.

### Two records for the same customer

It happens — someone calls from a second number and gets entered twice.

1. Open one of them.
2. Tap **Merge**.
3. Choose the record to keep.
4. Confirm.

Everything — enquiries, activities, contacts, projects — moves onto the record you
keep. **Merging cannot be undone**, so check both records first.

## 10. Practising safely

The training system is a complete copy of the CRM filled with invented customers.
Nothing you do there touches real data, and nothing you do there can reach a real
customer.

Practise:

- adding a customer and an enquiry
- logging a call and setting a follow-up
- moving an enquiry from **New** all the way to **Won**
- marking one **Lost** and choosing a reason
- attaching a photo
- as a manager, finding today's overdue follow-ups
- as the owner, opening each report

To start again from a clean set of practice data, ask whoever runs the server to
run the demo reset. It takes about a minute and gives everyone a fresh copy.

## 11. Quick answers

**I can't see a customer another salesperson added.**
That is correct. You see your own customers, plus anything you are working on.
Managers see their showroom; the owner sees everything.

**I made a mistake in an activity.**
You have 24 hours to fix it. After that, add a new note explaining the correction.

**A customer says they never got the quotation.**
Open the enquiry and look at the activity history — it shows what was sent and
when.

**I marked something Won by mistake.**
Ask a manager or the owner. They can reopen it. The record that it was once won
stays in the history, which is intentional.

**The system says this customer might already exist.**
Check before creating a second one. One customer with a full history is worth far
more than two half-empty records.

**Do I have to fill in every field?**
No. Name and phone number are enough to start. Everything else can be added as you
learn it — but **always set the next action and its date**.
